## Recommendation

Add six focused candidates to the page-buffer roadmap. Start with measurements under the existing performance-framework issue, then prioritize holder lookup and ordinary latch-waiter enqueue. Investigate asynchronous replacement maintenance separately from asynchronous flush execution, and centralize acquisition rollback before broadening concurrency changes.

## 1. Reduce per-thread holder lookup and removal cost

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] Reduce per-thread holder lookup and removal cost`

### Finding

The holder list is per thread. Let `H` be the number of distinct BCBs held by that thread. `pgbuf_find_thrd_holder()` performs an O(H) search; final removal of a non-head entry can require another traversal. New-holder insertion itself is O(1), because allocation prepends the holder to the active list.

Nested fixes of the same page reuse one holder and increment its `fix_count`; nested count does not grow the list. Acquiring N distinct pages without releasing earlier ones can accumulate O(N²) unsuccessful lookup work. Many threads fixing one page instead stress the shared BCB atomic state, even when each thread's holder list is short.

### Proposed scope

Compare the current representation with a small inline collection plus indexed overflow, or a per-thread hash plus iterable ownership list. Eliminate the second traversal during final removal where possible. An explicit acquisition handle is a larger interface alternative and should be evaluated separately.

Removing holder entries entirely requires replacing their ownership information. Nested acquisition, promotion, ordered watchers, and enumeration of held pages depend on it. A BCB-side reader map moves the information into shared state and introduces synchronization on the hot page; it does not remove the accounting obligation.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

- Measure hold-depth distributions, lookup comparisons, CPU per fix/unfix, and memory consumption.
- Compare shallow/deep holds, LIFO/non-LIFO release, and repeated access to an older held entry.
- Preserve nested ownership, promotion, ordered-watcher enumeration, cleanup, and allocation-failure behavior.
- Include the nested READ-to-WRITE accounting regression from [CBRD-27370](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27370) as a regression constraint.

Evidence: [holder structure, lifecycle, consumers, and cost](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/advanced/holder-entry-lifecycle.md); current `src/storage/page_buffer.c`, `pgbuf_allocate_thrd_holder_entry`, `pgbuf_find_thrd_holder`, and `pgbuf_unlatch_thrd_holder`.

## 2. Make ordinary page-latch waiter enqueue constant-time

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] Make ordinary page-latch waiter enqueue constant-time`

### Finding

An ordinary incompatible request walks from the BCB wait-queue head to its tail while holding the BCB mutex. Building a queue of N arrivals before drainage accumulates O(N²) traversal. The current source still contains the tail-search loop in `pgbuf_block_bcb()`.

This is separate from reader-group wakeup: the latter performs a forward scan and can grant readers across retained writers. The seminar does not support attributing quadratic queue construction to repeated reader-group grants.

### Proposed scope

Introduce constant-time ordinary append, with queue operations owning head/tail consistency, promoter head insertion, cancellation, grant/removal, and waiter flags. Keep scheduling policy unchanged in this issue. Any change to reader grouping or writer fairness needs its own explicit decision.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

- Ordinary enqueue performs constant traversal work as queue depth grows.
- Tail state remains correct after timeout, interrupt, promotion, reader-group removal, and flush-waiter removal.
- Measure BCB mutex hold time, contended-page latency, and the memory cost of additional per-BCB fields.
- Preserve waiter wakeup and flag consistency across all removal paths.

Evidence: [alternating reader/writer wakeup and enqueue cost](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/reference/alternating-reader-writer-wakeup-cost.md); current `src/storage/page_buffer.c`, `pgbuf_block_bcb()`.

## 3. Defer and coalesce replacement-policy updates from final unfix

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] [Survey] Defer and coalesce replacement-policy updates from final unfix`

### Finding

Final global unfix can move a BCB between private/shared lists, boost its position, and adjust zone boundaries while holding the BCB mutex. However, not every unfix moves a page: ordinary zone-1 hits keep their position, sufficiently young zone-2 hits skip boosting, and existing reader/writer waiters suppress ordinary policy work.

The relevant zero crossing is the BCB's global `fcnt == 0`, not merely one thread's holder `fix_count == 0`.

### Proposed scope

Start by comparing deferred resident-page promotion and migration against synchronous boost suppression. Evaluate initial `VOID -> LRU` admission separately, because postponing admission also postpones ordinary replacement discovery.

```text
Unfixer: consume ownership debt and perform required latch handoff
         record or coalesce replacement intent

Daemon:  validate current residency and policy state
         apply bounded batches of list maintenance
```

This is a proposed division of responsibility. It is not sufficient to enqueue a raw BCB pointer and replay the old decision later.

The survey must settle residency identity after BCB reuse; original private-domain context after the requesting thread moves on; refix and eviction races; coalescing semantics; overflow fallback; daemon delay; and shutdown draining. A delayed initial admission must not strand otherwise usable frames under pressure. Deferred policy may change victim order and miss rate even when ownership correctness is preserved.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

Measure unfix p99, total foreground-plus-daemon CPU, queue lag, LRU mutex contention, miss rate, and allocator progress under pressure. Compare hot resident probes, cyclic scans, mixed sessions, and dirty-buffer pressure. Establish a bounded-progress fallback when the daemon cannot keep up.

[CBRD-27191](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27191) limits its implementation to boost suppression and explicitly leaves batching for a later stage. Link this candidate as that follow-up rather than duplicating its present scope.

Evidence: [private-domain and final-unfix placement audit](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/reference/private-lru-domain-hit-age-and-unfix-placement.md); current `src/storage/page_buffer.c`, `pgbuf_unlatch_bcb_upon_unfix()`.

## 4. Move deferred page-flush execution off the unfixer

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] [Survey] Move deferred page-flush execution off the unfixer`

### Finding and proposed scope

An async-flush request can cause `pgbuf_unlatch_bcb_upon_unfix()` to invoke `pgbuf_bcb_safe_flush_force_unlock()` itself. The request's asynchronous character does not mean a daemon performs the eventual work. The unfix path also clears a returned flush error.

Investigate transferring eligible pending flush work to a daemon with explicit completion, retry, and failure ownership. Keep this separate from replacement-policy maintenance: page writes carry WAL, dirty-generation, and waiter-completion obligations that LRU hints do not.

The existing post-flush daemon offers a useful bounded-queue and fallback example, but it completes already-submitted flush bookkeeping; it is not already a general substitute for this unfix-triggered execution path.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

Measure unfix latency under dirty-page pressure. Verify WAL ordering, dirty-generation tracking, flush-waiter completion, checkpoint progress, queue saturation, I/O failure, and shutdown. Coordinate with [CBRD-27262](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27262) so execution transfer preserves flush-error state recovery.

Evidence: [daemon responsibilities and post-flush handoff](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/reference/page-buffer-daemon-lifecycle-audit.md); [VS-13](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/unresolved-or-version-sensitive-findings.md); current `pgbuf_unlatch_bcb_upon_unfix()`.

## 5. Centralize acquisition grant and ownership rollback

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] Centralize acquisition grant and ownership rollback`

### Finding and proposed scope

The seminar identifies paths where atomic latch/fix grant precedes holder allocation. Failure after the grant exposes a rollback obligation. The current READ fast path still visibly increments global `fcnt` before allocating a missing holder and can return `NULL` on allocation failure.

Create one ownership module responsible for completing or reversing acquisition bookkeeping across immediate, READ-fast-path, and awakened-waiter grants. Compare reserving holder capacity before grant against explicit rollback. The purpose is to concentrate the success/failure invariant and its verification in one place, not merely wrap existing branches.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

Inject allocation failure at the relevant grant paths and verify global fix counts, holders, waiter grants, and watcher state through fix/unfix behavior. No failed acquisition should leave unexplained ownership debt. Preserve behavior of successful nested acquisition and promotion.

The seminar labels this VS-11, a candidate requiring fault injection; this report does not claim a demonstrated production failure. Keep [CBRD-27370](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27370)'s concrete count regression independently deliverable.

Evidence: [acquisition and ownership ledger](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/learning/02-fix-hold-release.md); [VS-11](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/unresolved-or-version-sensitive-findings.md); current `pgbuf_lockfree_fix_ro()`.

## 6. Bound replacement-maintenance work and reduce failed victim scans

**Suggested title:** `[PGBUF] [Survey] Bound replacement-maintenance work and reduce failed victim scans`

### Finding and proposed scope

At the seminar baseline, a victim scan's 1,000-entry cap does not bound the entire operation. Zone adjustment can precede it and relabel additional BCBs. Failed scans, stale list advertisements, and maintenance work need separate accounting.

Measure rejection reasons, zone-demotion work, mutex hold time, and victim wait latency before choosing among bounded maintenance batches, improved candidate hints, and advertisement refresh policies. Revalidate the detailed scan path on the implementation target; this part of the proposal derives from pinned seminar evidence.

### Proposed acceptance evidence

Reduce work per successful victim and allocator tail latency under mixed dirty, pinned, and clean-page pressure. Preserve multi-session progress and evaluate workload fairness. Do not infer fairness from the existence of a scan cap.

Coordinate with [CBRD-27211](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27211), [CBRD-27264](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27264), and [CBRD-27316](http://jira.cubrid.org/browse/CBRD-27316).

Evidence: [victim-scan cap audit](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/reference/victim-scan-cap-and-aout-evidence.md); [replacement quantities and costs](https://github.com/vimkim/my-cubrid-page-buffer-seminar/blob/8dbdff4639734a2eeef327ba9a6343182548b09d/reference/replacement-policy-quantities-and-costs.md).

## Suggested execution sequence

1. Establish common measurements under CBRD-27423, separating holder traversal, atomic contention, waiter work, LRU movement, and flush execution.
2. Implement selected ordinary-enqueue and holder-lookup improvements with focused semantic regression coverage.
3. Establish acquisition rollback and failure-injection coverage.
4. Prototype async LRU maintenance and async flush transfer as separate changes, comparing total CPU, latency, misses, and progress.
5. Use measured replacement-pressure evidence to select candidate 6's implementation scope.

The `ask-matt` routing recommendation is architecture survey, then candidate selection and `grill-with-docs`, followed by a prototype where concurrency or performance needs a runnable answer. Turn the resulting decisions into specifications and implementation tickets only after the unresolved contracts and measurement criteria are settled.
